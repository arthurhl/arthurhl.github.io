04Proof, not buzzwords

What I like to
untangle.

A few real constraints I’ve had to respect — and the engineering choices that made them manageable.

01 / legacy performance
O(n²)
→ O(n)

Refactor the hot path before Azure becomes the bottleneck.

A heavily used company functionality was buried in legacy processing that degraded Azure performance. I cleaned the flow and reduced its algorithmic complexity from O(n²) to O(n), before the problem became an incident.

02 / database saturation
100%
no longer the norm

Rebuild the path, not just the symptom.

A company-wide monthly invoice used to push database utilization to 100%. Reworking the data, query, event, and caching path stopped the recurring saturation without changing the billing outcome.

03 / production in the field
Offline
by design

A field app that is still doing its job.

SUAS Mobile remains in production today, supporting social-assistance teams in cities including Serra and Cachoeiro de Itapemirim. Its offline-first SQLite flow syncs through REST when connectivity returns.

04 / government APIs
APIs
with consequences

Make bureaucracy legible to software.

At PJMEI, I integrated Brazilian government systems and APIs across finance, banking, education, and declaration flows — turning external rules into dependable product behavior.

05 / document fidelity
1:1
by necessity

When the document is the interface.

The architecture did not fully cover an unusually precise Airway Bill of Lading. I built an alternate document-generation path to reproduce its awkward details exactly — a document used heavily at Porto de Santos, Latin America's largest port.

06 / applied AI
RAG
multi-agent

Give the system more context, not more noise.

I designed a RAG architecture with multiple agents to build and document an entire system, expanding the context available to the work and increasing delivery velocity.
